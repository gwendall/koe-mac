# Changelog

What changed in each version of Koe, newest first. Also on
[koe.fm/changelog](https://koe.fm/changelog), in ten languages.

## 2.2.2 - 2026-10-16

- Your last words are no longer cut off. A knock on the desk could make Koe take softly spoken last words for silence; and after a tap, Koe now listens until your voice has gone quiet, not a fixed fifth of a second.
- Clearer Settings: a narrower shortcut button, each switch with its explanation in one row, your dictionary as tags you remove with ✕, and How Koe works at the end.

## 2.2.1 - 2026-10-15

- Koe asks before taking fn. If another app may start on fn too (Wispr Flow, Superwhisper, or any app listening to the keyboard), Try it asks you: keep fn for Koe, or use Right ⌥. An app that shows up later is pointed out once, and Settings offers the same switch.
- In Try it, if your shortcut never reaches Koe, Koe says another app may be taking it first, and how to pick another key.
- Update to Koe in the menu checks again before installing: on a copy that can't update itself, it sends you to koe.fm instead of failing.

## 2.2.0 - 2026-10-15

- A new menu, shared with Wham: Return after typing, Polish and Sounds tick on and off right in it, and the menu stays open; Check for Updates answers in place; About Koe shows your version. It's narrower, with your last dictations on one line each and how long ago on the right.
- Choose where the pill shows while you talk: Settings › Koe › Pill while you talk, at the bottom, top, left or right of the screen.
- Dictating in the middle of a sentence no longer starts with a stray capital: after a word or a comma, the first word stays lowercase (names, acronyms and “I” keep theirs).
- Setting up is sturdier: if Koe is missing from the Accessibility list, a card next to System Settings lets you drag it in, and the welcome window waits until it's on. Opened from the disk image, Koe offers to move itself to Applications first.
- Try it shows your first words with a check, right where you said them, and waits for Continue. If another dictation app that may use fn is open, Koe says so before it gets in the way.
- Smaller things: a thin scroll bar in Settings, and turning sounds off plays one last sound.

## 2.1.1 - 2026-10-15

- Polishing is easier to find and to follow: How Koe works, in Settings, says what it is and names the model; Koe tells you when it's ready after its download; and if the model can't start, Settings says so rather than blaming the connection.

## 2.1.0 - 2026-10-15

- New, off by default: Polish the words. A small language model on your Mac takes out repeats and corrections, spells your dictionary words and fixes punctuation, in about a tenth of a second. Turn it on in Settings: it downloads once (1.3 GB) in the background while Koe keeps typing.
- Filler sounds (um, uh, euh…) are now always taken out, with no delay.
- A dictionary word no longer replaces a real word that looks like it: with “wham” in your dictionary, “what” stays “what”.

## 2.0.2 - 2026-10-15

- The last words of a dictation are no longer cut off: Koe keeps listening a moment after you let go of fn.
- Escape cancels a dictation without reaching the app behind it: in a terminal, Claude Code no longer stops its work too.
- The pill at the bottom of the screen no longer flashes a wave after every dictation: it shows one only when the words take a moment.

## 2.0.1 - 2026-10-15

- Hold fn to talk, or tap it once to start and again to stop: both work, with no setting to choose.
- The welcome now ends with a little celebration, once your first words land in the try-it box.
- The first time, the welcome says when Koe is preparing its voice model for your Mac's Neural Engine, with a bar that moves, instead of a silent “Getting ready…”.

## 2.0.0 - 2026-10-15

- A new Koe, rebuilt from scratch as a native Mac app: a little chat bubble in your menu bar. Hold fn, talk, let go, and your words are typed where your cursor is.
- Everything happens on your Mac. Koe hears you with Parakeet Ultra on your chip's Neural Engine: no account, no server, and no connection needed once its voice model is downloaded.
- Much faster: a sentence is typed about a tenth of a second after you let go, and 30 minutes of audio takes about 4 seconds.
- 25 European languages, found on their own, even when you switch language in the middle of a dictation. The dictionary spells names and jargon your way.
- While you talk, a small candy pill sits low on the screen: gray bars while it waits for your voice, dancing bars as you talk, a wave while the words are worked out, a check when they land.
- Never loses a word: a dictation is on disk from its first second, and after a crash Koe puts it on the clipboard.
- Koe speaks your Mac's language: English, French, Spanish, German, Italian, Portuguese, Japanese, Korean and Chinese, Simplified and Traditional.
- Koe 2 does one thing, on your Mac: no cloud modes, no Voice Actions, no model to choose. It needs an Apple silicon Mac with macOS 14 or later; on an Intel Mac, keep Koe 0.1.
